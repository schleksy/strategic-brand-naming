# Research sources and execution

Use this guide before researching a project. Choose sources for the actual industry, market, and selected name types. These are research starting points, not claims that any source is accessible or any name is available today. Prefer free accessible sources; disclose a blocked page and use an appropriate alternative rather than claiming to have read it.

## Build source-backed territories

1. Translate the brief into actions, problems, outcomes, stakes, and audience language. Map direct and adjacent brands from their actual websites and product descriptions.
2. For a full project, compare multiple genuinely different territories, including a lateral world outside category vocabulary. Decide depth from the brief and findings, not a fixed quota of languages, territories, or hundreds of names. History, people, institutions, literature, objects, mechanisms, and older language are ways to find unexpected material. Do not force a historical or foreign-language name when the selected direction excludes it.
3. Search for real terminology, practices, people, artifacts, and roots. Follow promising citations to source material. Open and read the relevant entry or passage. Search snippets, AI summaries, name generators, and unsourced lists are leads, not evidence.
4. Keep a root card for promising material: exact word/reference; source URL and entry/passage; literal meaning or historical role; pronunciation; alternate meanings/baggage; connection to the business; possible naming constructions; date inspected. Separate the source's factual claim from the agent's creative analogy.
5. Develop multiple roots within each useful territory before construction. Record why weak territories were stopped or changed. Do not claim a candidate count or research depth that was not actually tracked.

## Source routes

| Research question | Start with | What to extract and verify |
|---|---|---|
| What do customers and practitioners actually call the behavior? | Customer-supplied research, product docs, professional associations, trade publications, public practitioner discussions, and industry glossaries. | Real language, precise function, emotional stakes, and category clichés. Treat anecdotes as anecdotes. |
| Which objects or mechanisms perform an analogous function? | University resources, museum collections, technical manuals, standards bodies, scientific papers, professional reference works. | The actual mechanism and why the analogy fits; do not invent technical explanations. |
| Which historical people, practices, institutions, or artifacts embody it? | [Library of Congress collections](https://www.loc.gov/collections/), [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/), relevant museum/university archives, relevant municipal cultural-history resources, original texts and scholarly editions. For example, use a city heritage authority to investigate a local public-commentary practice, then follow its historical references. | Actual role, period, meaning, pronunciation, and baggage. Trace summaries back to evidence. |
| Which literary or mythological references genuinely fit? | Original works, scholarly editions, university collections such as [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/), and reliable specialist scholarship. | The passage and what it supports. Separate later retellings and interpretation from the original. |
| What are an English word's older forms and semantic history? | [Oxford English Dictionary](https://www.oed.com/) when accessible, [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/), [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/), historical dictionaries and academic language resources. | Attested root, sense changes, pronunciation, and chronology. Do not claim an inaccessible entry was checked. |
| What does a classical or regional root actually mean? | [University of Chicago Logeion](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/), scholarly dictionaries and lexicons, national language academies, native-language dictionaries such as [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/) for Italian or [SJP PWN](https://sjp.pwn.pl/) for Polish, and relevant linguistic corpora. | Exact script, lemma, grammatical form, literal meaning, pronunciation/transliteration, and current associations. Corroborate a central unusual claim in another reliable source where possible. |
| What cultural or professional meaning does it carry today? | Current native-language sources, audience publications, relevant professional communities, and actual market usage. | Whether the association is current, widespread, sensitive, or only local. Distinguish observation from native-speaker validation. |

Use general encyclopedias and collaborative dictionaries to discover leads when necessary, then verify consequential claims in stronger sources. A thesaurus may expand vocabulary but cannot replace historical, cultural, or mechanism research. Choose additional sources appropriate to the project; this table is not a closed whitelist or a quota to visit every site.

## Discover roots, not just synonyms

Follow this pattern with the project's own subject:

**Underlying behavior → analogous practice/system → people, objects, stories, and vocabulary associated with it → verified roots → name constructions → intrinsic knockout → live qualification.**

For a product concerned with public criticism, for example, investigate the history of public commentary, satire, civic communication, and the physical places or artifacts used to publish it. Find the actual terms and source passages before inventing a name. The same method may lead a logistics product toward continuity practices, transfer rituals, relays, or physical joining systems. These illustrate research routes, not reusable winning names or verified project findings.

Explore adjacent senses and forms when an unusual root is promising. Compare the original word, a readable short form, and any deliberate compression. A quick assertion that a word is hard to read is a desk hypothesis, not an observed audience test. Revisit shelved material when a revised form or comparison makes it stronger, without overlooking genuine usability failures.

Do not build a story first and hunt for a source to justify it later. An attractive historical reference is still a weak candidate if the word is hard to say, crowded, or irrelevant.

## Check surviving candidates

Complete this sequence for each name that passes intrinsic knockout. Do not screen hundreds of raw fragments; do not present survivors as recommendations before screening them.

1. **Company and product use.** Search `"ExactName"`, unquoted forms, spacing/hyphen variants, close spellings, likely phonetic variants, and name plus category/market. Read actual company/product pages. Add app stores, software/package directories, sector directories, and relevant official business registers, such as [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/) for UK companies or the appropriate state/country registry. A company-register result and a trademark result answer different questions. If results are crowded or contradictory, deepen the search and use another search engine when available.
2. **Exact domain.** Use [ICANN Lookup](https://lookup.icann.org/) or the relevant registry's RDAP service for current registration data. For extensions not covered, use the registry's official lookup. Check a live registrar's exact-domain result for whether it can actually be purchased, at what observed price, and whether it is standard registration, premium registration, resale, auction, or brokerage. An RDAP no-object response alone is not a registrar quote or proof a registry will sell a reserved name. A timeout, blocked request, missing DNS record, or search absence means unknown. Check IDN/punycode and lookalike ambiguity when relevant. If exact `.com` is a hard requirement, an alternate extension or a `get` prefix does not pass it.
3. **Preliminary trademarks.** Use [USPTO Trademark Search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) for the US; [UK IPO](https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark) for the UK; [TMview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/) and the relevant official office for European markets; and the relevant national office elsewhere. [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database) can broaden research but does not replace relevant national/regional searches or cover every mark worldwide. Search exact, close spelling, phonetic, and relevant meaning variants in related goods/services. Inspect actual records and statuses. Record search scope and identifiers; do not call a no-hit search legal clearance. See [USPTO's search guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search) and [likelihood of confusion](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion) for the distinction.
4. **Language and cultural usability.** Check the actual construction in relevant languages and markets, including unwanted soundalikes, slang, political associations, and misleading etymology. State when pronunciation is a desk assessment or cultural nuance needs a native speaker.
5. **Search and social use.** Check dominant meanings, search confusion, relevant platform profiles, and required exact handles. A platform's blocked or missing profile page may be ambiguous. Report unknown unless the platform provides enough evidence.

Keep an evidence row for each check: exact name/query/domain; source/result URL or database and record identifier; actual check date; market and goods/services scope; observed result; limitation; pass/fail/unknown; next action. Preserve negative findings and failed candidates. Never collapse domain registration, company registration, trademark risk, and social availability into a single "available" flag.

## Finish the work

- Use available tools through discovery, generation, knockout, and external screening. Do not give a research plan as a substitute for performing accessible checks.
- Record actual work by territory and batch: roots investigated, constructions generated, names presented, names rejected with reasons, and survivors checked. Keep these counts distinct and mark unknown historical counts as unknown. More output is not evidence of better research.
- Reject hard failures and research replacements. If none survive, report that and identify the next territory or constraint decision. Never quietly relax the user's brief.
- Try suitable alternative sources when a service is inaccessible. Do not bypass access restrictions, buy services, register domains, or file trademarks as part of research.
- Treat inaccessible official databases as incomplete checks even if search-engine snippets exist. Show a checked candidate's unresolved fields honestly; do not promote it to a screened finalist.
- If live research is unavailable, provide completed discovery and any supported territory findings, then explain what remains unknown. Show unchecked names only when the user explicitly requests exploratory ideas. Never imply name or domain availability.
- Recheck relevant availability before the final recommendation and again before a launch/purchase decision if time has passed. Source access and search findings can change.
