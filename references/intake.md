# Business and naming intake

Use this for new naming projects. Reuse facts the user has already supplied, including reliable project documents. Ask only unanswered material questions, but do not skip the business brief to start brainstorming. In the first intake response, ask missing core questions about the business and industry, audience/differentiation, desired impression, markets, name-type direction, and domain requirements together. Include the short name-type menu in that first response. Use a later round for deeper follow-ups rather than postponing those core choices.

## Understand the business

Group questions naturally around these areas:

1. **Business and industry:** What exactly are we naming: company, product, service, feature, or umbrella brand? Explicitly ask which industry, subsector, and category it is in or serves. Labels such as AI, app, or B2B do not answer the industry question. Describe what it does in plain English and give a concrete example of someone using or buying it. Is it a new business or a rename? For a rename, what should be retained or left behind?
2. **Offering and business model:** What problem does it solve, how does it work, and what changes for the customer? What makes it different from the main alternatives? What is actually being sold, at what positioning or price level, and how is it bought? Skip pricing details only when they cannot affect naming.
3. **People and competition:** Who uses it, who pays, and who must trust it? Which industries or customer segments matter? Who are the main competitors or substitutes? Request links or existing materials when available, but do not require a website for a new business.
4. **Brand and growth:** What three qualities should the name suggest? What should it never suggest? Which existing brand names do you like or dislike, and why? What future products, audiences, or markets must the name accommodate?

Research public facts independently when useful. A competitor page can establish what the competitor sells; it cannot establish the user's business strategy or preferences.

## Choose name types and practical constraints

Explicitly offer this menu, with short explanations. Ask which types to prioritize and which to avoid. Accept one type, several ranked types, an open mix, or an explicit request for guidance. If the user is unsure, explain the tradeoffs and ask whether to explore the recommended mix. Do not infer their preference from their industry.

| Type | Plain-English explanation |
|---|---|
| Descriptive | Directly says what the business offers or does. |
| Suggestive / evocative | Hints at a benefit, behavior, or feeling. |
| Metaphorical | Borrows an image, object, or system that parallels the business. |
| Arbitrary / abstract | Uses an existing word unrelated to the category. |
| Coined / invented | Creates a new word or combines meaningful roots. |
| Founder / eponymous | Uses a person's name. |
| Geographic / cultural | Draws on a place, tradition, or cultural reference. |
| Acronym / initialism | Uses letters from a longer phrase. |

Explain that industry terminology, historical research, and foreign-language roots are research territories; blends, compounds, and compressed spellings are construction methods. These can support more than one name type. Coined and abstract are not interchangeable.

Resolve these constraints before generating:

- Launch and expansion countries; languages the audience reads and speaks; relevant trademark jurisdictions and intended goods/services.
- Whether exact `.com` is mandatory, other allowed extensions or modifiers, and whether buying an existing/premium domain is acceptable. Ask the purchase budget if resale is acceptable. Record an explicit "undecided" rather than assuming flexibility.
- Required social platforms/handles, if any; one word versus multiple words; length or syllable limits; pronunciation and spelling; acceptable wordplay, abbreviations, and altered spelling.
- Excluded words, associations, languages, naming styles, existing roots, and category clichés. Separate hard exclusions from softer preferences.
- Any parent-brand relationship, existing marks, restricted terminology, or need to work in regulated/professional contexts.

## Completion rule

Record each material area as **answered**, **explicitly open/delegated**, or **unanswered**. Do not assume "not mentioned" means "no preference."

Before a full naming round, require:

- The business object, industry/subsector, offering/difference, and audience/buyer.
- Markets/languages, desired impression and exclusions, future scope.
- A selected type direction or explicit permission to explore a mix.
- Domain requirements and other hard usability constraints, including explicit undecided answers where applicable.

Summarize the brief, type direction, success criteria, and disqualifiers. Proceed without an extra approval round once answers resolve these points. Ask targeted follow-ups when the answers are too vague to guide decisions. Do not repeat the entire questionnaire for a pivot or a request to check one existing name.

If the user explicitly requests quick ideas without completing discovery, identify the assumptions and produce only exploratory output. A short clarification or progress update is allowed before screening; a recommended naming shortlist is not.
